Storyboard

Who the story is for, what they want to know, and how the website walks them from question to answer.

Audience

Who

Australian households about to buy a TV. Not data experts; they know the energy label but not how screen technology affects it.

What they want to know

Will an OLED or LED LCD cost more to run than an LCD? Is it the technology, or something else? What should I compare?

How they read

Quickly, often on a phone or laptop. They need one clear message per chart and plain-English text.

Guidelines for the story

  • One message per chart, with an insight sentence underneath.
  • Show the unfair raw comparison first, then correct for screen size.
  • Keep one colour per technology across all charts.
  • Flag small groups and limits of the data honestly.
  • End with a simple action: pick a size, then compare kWh per year.

User journey

Scene 1: Hook (Home page)

Reader question
Does the type of TV change my power bill?
Content
Hero question, three key numbers (4,839 models, 296 to 422 kWh, about 4%), FAQ.
Design choice
Show the surprising contrast (big raw gap, tiny real gap) as numbers before any chart.

Scene 2: Setup (Figure 2)

Reader question
What is actually on sale?
Content
Bar chart of model counts. LCD (LED) is 81% of the market.
Design choice
Simple bar chart; sorted, labelled directly, so no explanation is needed.

Scene 3: Conflict (Figure 3 and table)

Reader question
Is LCD cheaper to run?
Content
Box plot of kWh per year. LCD median 296 against 408 and 422. Table reveals LCD TVs are 14 inches smaller on average.
Design choice
Let the reader believe the obvious answer first, then break it with the size column.

Scene 4: Twist (Figure 4)

Reader question
What if the screens were the same size?
Content
Median kWh per 100 cm²: 4.11, 4.16 and 4.00. The gap shrinks to about 4%.
Design choice
Same chart type as the earlier one, so the change in the result is easy to see.

Scene 5: Proof and caution (Figures 5 to 7)

Reader question
Does it hold in real size ranges, in use, and for stars?
Content
Size-band comparison, in-use power and star share. Technologies are close except at very large sizes.
Design choice
Warn about small groups so the reader does not over-trust sparse bars.

Scene 6: Call to action (Conclusion)

Reader question
So what should I do?
Content
Pick the size you need, then compare kWh per year on the label for models of that size.
Design choice
One short, bold paragraph; no new numbers.
